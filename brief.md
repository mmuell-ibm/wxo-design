# SkyConnect Airlines: The AI Revolution Project

## The Challenge

SkyConnect Airlines CEO Maria Santos slammed her laptop shut after reviewing the latest customer satisfaction scores. 3.2 out of 5 stars. Twenty-three minute average wait times. And customer service costs climbing 15% year over year despite their growth.

"We're hemorrhaging money and goodwill," she announced to the leadership team. "Every other airline is racing toward AI agents, and we're still playing catch-up with basic phone systems. I want us to leapfrog the competition."

The mandate was clear: build an AI-first customer service system that handles 80% of inquiries without human intervention, but make it feel premium—not like the "phone tree from hell" that passengers hate. The engineering team has six months to prove this concept works.

You've been brought in as the multi-agent system architects. Your job is to design, build, and test agents that can handle real customer conversations with the same quality as their best human agents, but at scale and speed that humans simply can't match.

## The Reality Check

The VP of Customer Experience, James Kim, pulls you aside after the kickoff meeting. "Look, I've seen a lot of AI projects crash and burn because they promised the world. These conversations get messy fast. Passengers are stressed, rules are complex, and one wrong move tanks our brand reputation."

He slides you a stack of call transcripts. "These are real conversations from last month. Some went great, some were disasters. Your agents need to handle the good, the bad, and the weird. And whatever you build, it better work when someone's connecting flight is boarding in ten minutes."

The transcripts reveal the complexity ahead. Frequent flyers who speak in airline jargon and expect instant service. Nervous first-time international travelers who need everything explained twice. Budget-conscious students trying to hack the system for better deals. And through it all, a maze of policies, fees, partner airlines, and government regulations that even experienced agents sometimes get wrong.

## Story 1: Flight Rebooking Capability

*Call transcript from March 15, 2024 - Sarah Chen (Gold Status Member)*

**Sarah:** "Hi, I just got a text that my 6:30 AM flight from Boston to Chicago is cancelled. I need to get on the next available flight."

**Agent:** "I'm sorry about the cancellation, Sarah. Let me pull up your booking and find you another flight to Chicago today... I can see you're a Gold member on flight SK 447. I'm checking available options now."

**Sarah:** "What do you have? I really need to get there today."

**Agent:** "I found two options: there's a 10:15 AM departure that gets you there at 1:30 PM, and a 1:45 PM departure arriving at 4:50 PM. Since this was a weather cancellation, there won't be any change fees. Which works better for you?"

**Sarah:** "The 10:15 AM flight sounds good. Can you book me on that?"

**Agent:** "Absolutely. I'm booking you on the 10:15 AM flight now. I can give you seat 15A which is an aisle seat like your original preference. Your new confirmation number is ABC123 and I'm sending the boarding pass to your phone right now."

## Story 2: Seat Change Request Capability

*Call transcript from February 8, 2024 - Michael Rodriguez (Family of 4)*

**Michael:** "Hi, I booked seats for my family on our London flight next week, but we're all scattered around the plane. Can you move us together? My kids are 8 and 12 and I don't want them sitting alone."

**Agent:** "I understand wanting to keep the family together, Michael. Let me pull up your booking and see what seat options we have available... I can see you currently have seats 12A, 15F, 22C, and 31B. Let me check what family seating is available."

**Michael:** "Yeah, that's terrible. We need at least the kids next to one of us adults. Are there any groups of seats together?"

**Agent:** "I'm checking the seat map now... I found a block of four seats together in row 18 - that's 18A, B, C, and D. I can move your whole family there. There is a $25 per person seat change fee since these are preferred seats, but I can see you're a Silver member so I can waive that fee."

**Michael:** "Perfect! Are any of those seats on the aisle? My wife gets claustrophobic in middle seats."

**Agent:** "Yes, seat 18A is an aisle seat. I'm processing the changes now and you'll receive updated boarding passes on your mobile app."
